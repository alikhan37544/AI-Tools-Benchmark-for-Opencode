The $10 question

What Go actually is

Go's per-token prices are essentially identical to going direct. The $10 buys a bundle of per-model prepaid allowances, each with its own 5-hour (20%), weekly (50%) and monthly (100%) cap. The multiplier ranges from 1.5× on the $15 premium rations to 8× on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and 6× on the Flash-tier models.

That is why “29 models” matters: you are not buying one $300 pot. You are buying 29 small, non-transferable pots.

What Go is not

  • Not unlimited. K3.1 Pro is ~269 heavy requests/month; Argon ~728; GLM-5.3 ~279.
  • Not a frontier pass. Opus 5.5 (AA 58), GPT-6 Astra (53) and Muse Wonder (52–56) are outside Go.
  • Not private by default for Muse. Muse Spark 1.3/1.2 Contributor still trains; everything else is “Not used”.
  • Not India-neutral. Go is the only plan here with documented UPI. Alibaba and Meta Muse Code fail the India gates.

Three findings that change decisions

1. Argon is the independent agentic leader on Go

Vals v2.2 puts Argon at 69.24% — third overall behind Sonnet 5.5 (70.85) and Opus 5.5 (70.18), and first among Go models. It is 6.7 points ahead of K3.1 Pro on that board, while AA puts K3.1 Pro 2.1 points ahead on general intelligence. Different questions, different winners: Argon for agentic execution, K3.1 Pro for raw reasoning.

2. The cheap tier got genuinely good

MiniMax M3.1 Flash scores AA 43 at $0.22/AA task — five points above DeepSeek's conservative 39 and 4 AA points above GLM-5.3-Flash (42), at a lower list price. The real shift is quality: GLM-5.3-Flash (42), Qwen3.8 Flash (40) and M3.1 Flash (43) now clear the old AA 39 workhorse line at 4.9k–9.8k req/mo. DeepSeek remains the cheapest per Go request ($0.0031 vs $0.0040), so it stays the volume driver.

3. Vendor scores and independent scores disagree hardest on TB4.0

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash wins vendor TB2.1 at 90.6 but scores 26.8 on AA's standardized TB4.0. GLM-5.3-Flash goes 84.3 vendor → 62.9 Vals. Treat any single harness number as a range, and prefer models with two independent strong results.

4. The AA ceiling is now 3 points from Opus

K3.1 Pro (55.1) sits between Opus 5.5 (58) and Argon (53). At $2.11 per AA task it is the most expensive per unit of work on Go — but it is the first Go model that does not require a frontier escape hatch for hard architecture.

The three gates, explained

Gate 1 — country at signup

Alibaba Cloud Model Studio Token Plan (Personal) is documented Singapore region only. Alibaba's international signup requires the phone country to match the selected region, and India is not selectable for this plan. Do not use a foreign number or mismatched region — that risks account closure and refund loss. Gate 2 — phone verification

OpenCode, Z.ai, StepFun and MiniMax have no phone gate documented. Xiaomi's overseas path uses email, Google or Facebook — the docs explicitly separate mainland users (mobile number) from overseas users (email). A Chinese phone is not required. Kimi accepts international numbers; +91 is the expected path but not published as a country list.

Gate 3 — payment

UPI works only on OpenCode Go/Plus. Indian Visa/Mastercard works almost everywhere; RuPay international acceptance varies, and recurring USD mandates are sometimes declined even when the first charge succeeds. PayPal is the Z.ai fallback. Waffo USD covers Xiaomi (Apple/Google Pay, card). Keep a second card and expect 3-D Secure OTP + ~2–3.5% FX markup.

Gate 4 — model access

OpenCode serves all models through its US gateway, but two settings bite: DeepSeek → Regions = Global (default is Europe & US, so it can look blocked), and Muse → “Allow paid endpoints that train on request data.” Beam's exclusion list does not include India. Gemini API lists India. xAI's global endpoint is the default. Alibaba/Qwen models remain reachable through Go even though the Token Plan is not sold to India.

Go vs going direct — per-model break-even

The one-sentence answer: Go's per-token prices are essentially the same as direct for almost every model. Reading the multiplier

1.5× — convenience, not a deal

Most $15-cap models (K3.1 Pro, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, Qwen Max, Grok, Step 5, Haiku, GPT). Go is a modest 50% discount. Fine if you already want several of them; not a reason to consolidate a single-model workflow.

2×–3× — a real discount

M3.1 Flash (2×), Argon (3×), Qwen Flash (3×), Hy4 (3×). Worth using Go even if you mostly want that one model, provided you are above its break-even.

6×–8× — the reason Go exists

DeepSeek (8×), MiMo-Flash, GLM-Flash, Hy3, LongCat, M3, K2.7, Muse (6×). These allowances are where $10 buys $60–$80 of list-price usage. The multiplier is not transferable — that is why breadth matters.

When direct wins

One model below its break-even; a model Go does not carry (Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1); provider features (batch, fine-tuning, caching controls, dedicated rate limits, data residency, ZDR contracts, invoices); compliance isolation; or a specialist plan that beats Go on its single model (Z.ai for GLM, Xiaomi below 3k MiMo-Flash, StepFun if credits hold).
